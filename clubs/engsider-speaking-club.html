Engsider Speaking Club

Engsider Speaking Club is the speaking practice run by Engsider, an English school that works mostly with people in IT — developers, analysts, product folks who need English for interviews and for everyday work with colleagues. 🗣️ The meetings themselves are ordinary, friendly conversation on a topic of the week — what's different is the crowd, who mostly come from the same industry.

It is an online club with a native-speaker host, Dan, and there are two time slots every week, so you can take the one that fits your calendar. Every Monday the club sets the topic and sends out the vocabulary and cheat sheets, so nobody arrives cold.

The school is refreshingly honest about what the club is not: it won't dig through your technical experience or drill you with stress questions — that's their separate one-to-one training. What it does do is take the panic out of speaking, so the small talk at the start of an interview stops being the scariest part of it.

Our Format

Format: a monthly subscription with four live online meetings a month — one topic per week — plus materials and a closed chat where the practice carries on between sessions.

  • 🎤 Host — Dan, a native speaker, University of Warwick graduate with 15+ years of teaching
  • 📅 Topic of the week every Monday, with vocabulary and cheat sheets sent in advance
  • 🕒 Two slots a week — the same topic twice, come to whichever suits you
  • 💬 A closed chat with the other members, where you can practise and ask questions any day
  • 🎯 Level B1–B2+ — comfortable from B1, and you're welcome to come just to listen

💬 Past topics: "Setting Goals", "Is AI Changing Society?", "Life Hacks and Mind Hacks", "Lies, Scams and Cons". One October meeting turned into a haunted-house quest — everyone became ghost hunters, made up a ghost story and a horror-movie plot, then argued about the perfect Halloween playlist. 🎃

After the first meeting with Dan the host wrote that nobody sat it out in a corner — everyone joined the discussion. In the participants' own words, the atmosphere was "supportive and encouraging, no fear of speaking".
